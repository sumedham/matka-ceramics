## The patterns

**Lowercase openings.** Almost every caption starts lowercase. Capitalising the
first word immediately sounds like someone else wrote it.

**No closing full stop.** Captions stop rather than end.

**Tildes as emphasis.** `~link in bio~`, `~the spread~`. This is hers. Use it
sparingly, the way she does, roughly one caption in four.

**Glaze described by what it reminds her of, never by technique.** "sunlight
hitting deeper ocean waters", "it's giving antelope canyon". She never writes
"reduction fired" or "iron saturate". The comparison is the whole caption.

**Casual internet register.** "it's giving", "super excited", "getting an itch".

**Anticipation.** She frequently trails what is coming: "next week", "soon",
"in the works".

**Credits the photographer**: `pc: @nidshoots`, lowercase.

**Typos survive.** "shapes that around round". Do not polish her. An overly
clean caption is the clearest tell that it was not hers.

**Length: very short to one run-on sentence.** Never a paragraph. Never a
line-broken list. Never an emoji, in any caption sampled.

## Hashtags

Always five, always the last line, no lead-in text.

- **Always present:** `#ceramics` `#southasian`
- **Rotates through:** `#handmade` `#pottery` `#clay` `#handmadepottery`
  `#interior` `#interiordesign` `#homeinteriors` `#kitchendesign`
  `#blockprint` `#blockprinted` `#bespoke` `#candle` `#kollam` `#diwali`

## Spelling, which matters

She writes **"kollam"** (and once "kollum"). The website currently says
**"Kolam print candle"**. Hers is the spelling that should win on her own shop.
Worth raising with her rather than silently changing, since it is a real word
with more than one transliteration.

## A caution on comments

Several comments are engagement bait: "Send me this post", "Can you please send
it?" from unfamiliar accounts. Do not read comment counts as real interest, and
do not imitate whatever those posts did.
